# Vision

Manipulation Score exists to make manipulative influence easier to see without pretending software can read minds, diagnose people, or settle the truth of a relationship.

Clarity, the message-analysis product, helps a person slow down, inspect observable language, understand which pressure functions may be present, and choose a grounded next step. The product provides evidence and reflection space. The person keeps authority over meaning, context, and action.

## Who we serve

Clarity primarily serves an individual privately reviewing a message or conversation they are authorized to examine. It also supports education, fictional scenarios, and authorized organizational training when material is consented to or de-identified.

The product is not built for spectators scoring public figures, employers evaluating workers, institutions making punitive decisions, or anyone profiling named people without consent.

## The outcome we create

After using Clarity, a person should be better able to answer:

- Which observable language functions were detected?
- Where is the evidence in the message?
- How might those functions narrow informed and voluntary choice?
- How much can be concluded from the available text?
- What can I pause, clarify, verify, or say next?

The desired outcome is greater clarity and agency, not a more confident accusation.

## The responsibility we own

Clarity owns the analysis of observable pressure-related language in the material supplied. It must connect every result to visible evidence, explain the relevant function in plain language, communicate uncertainty, and offer response options that preserve user choice.

Clarity does not determine intent, personality, diagnosis, truth, abuse, relationship safety, or whether a person is a manipulator. It does not replace the user's context, professional advice, or an emergency assessment.

## Evidence comes before a score

A number is useful only as a compact summary of evidence the user can inspect. Highlighted language, detected functions, context handling, and limitations are more important than the score itself.

Clarity should never produce a precise-looking verdict without showing what contributed to it. Product decisions that make the score more prominent while making evidence or uncertainty harder to inspect conflict with the product's purpose.

## Analyze communication, not identity

The unit of analysis is a specific message, interaction, article, advertisement, interface, speech, or campaign artifact. The product describes observed language functions in that material.

It does not convert an artifact-level result into a label about a person's character. It does not rank named individuals, diagnose personality disorders, infer hidden intent, or declare someone manipulative or abusive.

## Uncertainty must be visible

Context, relationship history, tone, culture, irony, quotation, and missing messages can change meaning. Clarity must acknowledge those limits where the user encounters the result, not bury them in a legal page.

When the input is insufficient, the responsible output is abstention. When evidence is mixed, the product should represent ambiguity rather than forcing a stronger band. Regression tests are evidence of software consistency, not proof of real-world accuracy.

No sensitivity, specificity, fairness, or accuracy claim should be published without representative validation that supports that exact claim.

## Privacy is the default architecture

Private communications are unusually sensitive. Analysis should remain on the user's device by default, with no account, upload, analytics, or third-party runtime request required for the core experience.

Any future feature that transmits or stores message content requires a clear user benefit, explicit informed consent, data minimization, a defined retention policy, and a safer alternative when practical. Growth, personalization, or convenience alone does not justify weakening this boundary.

## Safety is separate from manipulation scoring

Language involving violence, confinement, stalking, weapons, or self-harm coercion may require a safety notice rather than a manipulation score. This separation prevents an ordinary score from appearing to measure danger.

A safety notice must state that Clarity cannot determine whether someone is in danger and should direct the user toward appropriate resources without claiming certainty. Safety logic should prioritize clear action and careful context handling over dramatic language.

## The user retains agency

Response suggestions are starting points for pausing, setting a boundary, or asking for clarification. They should remain adaptable and should not escalate conflict by default.

Clarity should not tell a user what another person intended or dictate whether to stay, leave, confront, report, or punish. Where risk or professional judgment matters, it should help the user recognize the limit and seek appropriate human support.

## Misuse resistance is a product requirement

Clarity must resist public scoring, harassment, doxxing, mass profiling, punitive decision-making, and the use of its output as proof in legal, employment, housing, educational, disciplinary, or clinical settings.

Acceptable-use boundaries should be supported by product design, not only policy language. Features such as public leaderboards, named-person profiles, automated bulk analysis, or share cards that imply a person-level verdict conflict with this vision.

## Calm, accessible explanation builds trust

The experience should feel grounded, private, and non-accusatory. Plain language, readable evidence, keyboard access, appropriate contrast, and reduced-motion support are part of analytical quality.

The interface should create reflection space rather than urgency. Alarmist visuals, sensational copy, and engagement mechanics that reward repeated suspicion undermine the product.

## Scope and non-goals

Clarity is an educational message-analysis and manipulation-literacy tool. It is not a clinical instrument, lie detector, safety assessment, legal evidence generator, employee screening system, public reputation score, or automated judge of people.

Manipulation Score may eventually support other artifacts and educational experiences, but every product must preserve evidence linkage, uncertainty, consent, privacy, and human decision authority.

## Decision tests

A change fits this vision when it:

- Helps a user understand observable influence tactics and their evidence.
- Increases clarity without overstating what the supplied material can prove.
- Preserves private, voluntary, and authorized use.
- Strengthens abstention, context handling, safety separation, or transparent validation.
- Leaves consequential judgments with the user and appropriate human professionals.

We resist a change when it:

- Labels, ranks, diagnoses, or publicly scores named people.
- Makes a numeric score more authoritative than its evidence warrants.
- Sends or stores private content without a necessary, explicit, and understandable choice.
- Enables mass profiling, punishment, harassment, or use as proof of wrongdoing.
- Uses fear, outrage, or certainty as an engagement or monetization strategy.
