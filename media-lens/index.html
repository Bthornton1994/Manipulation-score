Analyze one public article

Media Lens analyzes one public page from a short list of sites the operator approves. It keeps language, claims, coverage, and source context separate, and it links each finding to the text it came from. It gives no score for the article, a person, or an outlet.

  • Media Lens does not yet find other reports of the same story or compare how outlets covered it. Examples on this page are made up and labeled as examples.
  • Clarity reviews a private message on your device. Open Clarity for that. Clarity does not use this workspace.

Before you analyze

Media Lens is for public material you are allowed to analyze, such as a published article, ad, speech, or campaign page. It is not the place for private messages or conversations; use Clarity for those instead. Clarity never sends your text anywhere. On the approved operator host, it can run a limited Jev-only live URL mode for public hosts on an operator allowlist. Live pasted-text analysis and the secondary classifier are disabled. Live URL access is limited, can be turned off by the operator, and is not production-ready for general use.

Before you analyze: in operator-hosted live mode, the approved operator host requests the public page at the URL you enter. The destination site (and its CDN) may see the operator host’s network address. Prepared public span text from that page may be sent to TypeSafe AI’s Jev service under TypeSafe’s own policy. TypeSafe does not fetch the URL. Full article text is not kept by Media Lens by default. Do not submit private messages, passwords, medical or financial records, information about children, paywalled content you are not authorized to fetch, or non-public/internal addresses. Live pasted-text analysis stays disabled. Use Clarity for private messages.

Coverage context

Coverage context is meant to show where a story started, which reports did their own reporting and which repeat another outlet's copy, and where coverage may have gaps.

Not live yet. Media Lens has no approved source for finding other reports of the same story, so an article analysis does not compare coverage.
